# TheBus Revision C • build and commissioning

Open the main-control and line-ring KiCad projects in the pcbs folder. Each root schematic is an index to its functional sheets. The line board has one sheet per eight-channel bank. Each project's connections.csv gives every pin on every component. Use the Revision C parts workbook and matching fabrication files for this pair of boards.

## Install and wire one prototype

1. Order four-layer, 1.6 mm FR-4 using one Gerber ZIP per board. Each ZIP includes separate plated and non-plated drill files. Provide the matching placement CSV and the workbook’s assembly tab. Verify pin 1, side and orientation for every optical package in the assembly preview. Q1–Q64 face the carpet. Keep flux, coating and adhesive off the optical windows.
2. Install the bare Teensy 4.0 over line-board J2/J3 on straight 2.54 mm pin strips, USB adjacent to both pad-1 ends. Set the underside of its module PCB 2.54 mm above the line PCB top for the supplied CAD allowance. Fit the Teensy 4.1 to the main-board sockets with VIN and GND at the labeled pad-1 ends. Use the Teensy VIN/VUSB isolation procedure on both boards for concurrent externally powered USB debugging. [PJRC Teensy 4.0](https://www.pjrc.com/store/teensy40.html) · [PJRC Teensy 4.1](https://www.pjrc.com/store/teensy41.html)
3. Connect the regulated 5 V rail to main J13 pin 1 and ground to pin 2. Power OpenMV N6 VIN through its own 5 V branch with common ground. Main J14 pins 1/2/3 are ground, to N6 P5/RX, and from N6 P4/TX. Servo or battery voltage on a signal pin will damage the logic. Main J16 mates to line J1, pin for pin. Use 1 = 5 V, 2 = ground, 5 = ring TX to main RX34, 6 = main TX35 to ring RX. Leave 3 and 4 empty. [N6 wiring reference](https://docs.openmv.io/dev/openmvcam/quickref/openmv-n6.html)
4. Connect the 3S pack through the 30 A main fuse close to the pack and the DC-rated master disconnect to the switched bus. Feed the four drive branches through individual 3 A fuses, the dribbler through 7.5 A, and the 5 V regulator through its 2 A input fuse. Fit the G2 local bulk capacitors and local 1 kΩ SLP pull-downs. Feed J18 pin 1 from the switched bus and pin 2 from its return. Fuse selection protects the harness. Validate motor current, wire ratings, temperature and stall protection under load. [G2 pinout and current feedback](https://www.pololu.com/product/2991)
5. Power the official referee module through the separate 1 A fused branch according to the module revision’s VIN specification. Route OUT1 through the isolated second master-switch pole to main J17 pin 2, with common ground on pin 1. Wire the maintained local RUN switch across J20 pins 1 and 2. Opening either the local RUN contact or referee permission removes the hardware permit. Probe actual drive enable on J3 pin 4. J19 pin 3 exposes the pre-timer RUN_OK signal. [Official referee-module wiring](https://github.com/robocup-junior/soccer-communication-module)
6. Main J3–J7 correspond to main J8–J12, in wheel 1 / wheel 2 / wheel 3 / wheel 4 / dribbler order. Driver pins are ground, PWM, direction, SLP, current sense and common active-low fault. Encoder pins are 5 V, ground, A and B. Supply MinIMU at 3.3 V on J15, then ground, SDA, SCL. Mount +Z upward. On J22 connect both beam heads to 3.3 V and ground, with receiver output on pin 2. The selected sensor reads low when the beam is broken. [Adafruit break-beam example](https://learn.adafruit.com/ir-breakbeam-sensors/arduino)
7. The ring consumes about 189 mA of emitter current in each illuminated half at a nominal 1.2 V LED drop. The two halves alternate. Measure supply droop with both Teensys, the camera and encoders active. With lifted wheels, verify master OFF, local STOP, referee STOP, unplugged signal harnesses and a stopped control loop. Measure actual SLP timeout with a scope. Verify that opening the local switch removes SLP even when the processor is frozen. A sleeping motor driver can coast.

## Calibrate from the main USB connection

Load LineNode.hex on the ring Teensy 4.0, MainControl.hex on the main Teensy 4.1, and OpenMV/main.py on the camera. The matching source sits alongside each image. Open the main USB serial terminal at 115200 baud with newline enabled. Keep local RUN open for setup.

| Step | Command and expected observation |
|---|---|
| Verify live inputs | Send status and sensors. Check referee/local permit, camera, ball gate, battery voltage and all five encoder counts. Send imu. Keep the robot still while the gyro collects 128 bias samples. A healthy camera sends fresh frames even when no orange candidate is present. |
| Calibrate the ring | Place all 64 windows above uniform green carpet at operating height. Send line green and wait for the command acknowledgment in line status. Repeat with line white on a uniform white target. Both masks must be healthy and the weak-channel mask zero. Send line save and verify saved=1. Power-cycle both boards and check again. Ring USB supports g, w, s, ? and r for raw measurements. |
| Calibrate mirror bearings | In OpenMV IDE set the ORANGE LAB threshold for the field and inspect the largest candidate. Send center X Y using the measured mirror center in the 320×240 image. Place the ball directly ahead of the chassis and send front. Move it to the robot’s right and send right. Keep the ball clearly displaced from the image center for both samples. |
| Identify wheel signs | Fit J3–J6 to shaft azimuths −135°, −45°, +45°, +135° from body +X, respectively, as viewed from above. Positive rolling tangents are −45°, +45°, +135°, −135°. With wheels raised, send jog N during STOP, then briefly permit PLAY within five seconds. Only motor N should run at low duty for at most 180 ms. Return to STOP. For each drive wheel use sign N D E where D is +1 if that jog produces positive body motion along its tangent, otherwise −1. E is +1 if turning the wheel in that positive convention increases its raw count, otherwise −1. Use sensors to observe counts. Verify the dribbler jog draws the ball in. |
| Store the main setup | Send save after registering the camera axes and four sign pairs. Confirm saved=1 in status and verify after a power cycle. An open J21 role strap selects the faster commissioning profile and a closed strap selects the slower profile. |
| Check auxiliary interfaces | Main aux exercises J23 UART7 and shows its state input. Ring USB p reads the flow sensor’s product register and motion pin and reports lidar/UART byte counts. Check exact voltage, pinout, baud and required driver for each optional peripheral. |

## Playing sequence and acceptance

During PLAY, valid line packets, fresh camera frames, a calibrated gyro, a healthy battery, a saved motion setup and clear driver faults permit motion. Absence of a ball candidate brings ordinary pursuit to rest. A line contact takes priority. The controller chooses a retreat direction using active sensor angles and the preceding motion, holds on an ambiguous stationary or all-white contact, and limits sustained retreat. It uses wheel encoders for speed feedback and gyro correction for heading. Ball possession commands the dribbler. Current feedback and a commanded wheel or roller that stops turning can trip the drive. After a trip, clear the cause, then cycle STOP and PLAY. Camera and ring stale timers are 200 and 100 ms respectively. Measure the resulting stopping distances at the actual speeds, including coasting.

Use the supplied drive-layout STEP with the floor at Z=0. Nominal optical gap is 1.50 mm and the lowest modeled ring feature clears a flat floor by 1.145 mm. The carrier is 2 mm thick with three 46 mm lower supports and four 10 mm upper spacers. Its underside is at 50.55 mm and the main PCB underside at 62.55 mm. The smallest drive-to-populated-ring gap is 1.25 mm. The added Teensy/USB and ring-plug envelopes clear the drives by at least 3.366 mm. These figures depend on the modeled assembly height. Screw heads, solder tails, brackets, plugs, wire bends, wheel play, carpet pile and tilt need a physical fit check. The assembled camera/mirror, battery, five G2 mounts, dribbler, cover and handle still need a complete chassis interference check.

Prototype one robot before duplicating it. Log per-channel contrast, bank faults, measured scan interval, missed lines and false retreats at several approach angles. Confirm ball bearings from at least eight directions, wheel signs in all translation directions, positive yaw correction in both directions, current readings against an ammeter and drive shutdown under each fault. Test the full operating envelope in the 180 mm gauge and measure capture depth with the roller installed. The included firmware supports low-speed ball pursuit, relative heading hold and line retreat. Goal recognition, long-term field localization, radio team strategy and powered kicking are outside that implemented behavior.
