# TheBus Revision B prototype

**Build one prototype before ordering both sets.** The board files pass the electronic design checks. The open fit questions are the wheel-to-ring gap, the mounted camera and mirror, and the fully wired robot inside the inspection cylinder. Start with `cad/drive-layout-study.step` and the two-robot parts workbook.
Use one matching main board and one matching line ring per robot. J16 on the controller mates to J1 on the ring through a straight-through keyed 10-pin IDC ribbon. Revision A used an eight-pin XH cable and remote sensors. Order from the Revision B workbook. The controller accepts a socketed Teensy 4.1, with the OpenMV N6 and five Pololu G2 drivers mounted separately.

## Boundary acquisition
The 32 onsemi QRE1113GR sensors face the floor. Their centers follow the existing board at a 55 mm radius. The +X axis in the STEP model is the angle datum. Looking down through the board, channel zero is 5.625° counterclockwise from +X. Add 11.25° for each successive channel. Optical Q1 reads channel zero and Q32 reads channel 31. Top silk numbers mark even channels. The halved angular pitch reduces the nearest-sensor angle bound from 11.25° to 5.625°. The actual contact-location error depends on carpet, paint, gap and motion.

Each mux address selects two neighbors. U1 reads even channels and U2 reads odd channels. U3 buffers both analog signals before the ribbon. Receiver collectors pull up to 3.3 V through individual 10 kΩ resistors. The 5 V supply drives 16 pairs of emitters through 220 Ω per pair and Q33 switches the shared return. At a typical 1.2 V per LED, all emitters together draw about 189 mA while lit. LED forward voltage, duty cycle and regulator drop change that number. Budget and measure it with the camera running.

The bench sketch in firmware/LineBench keeps the motors disabled. In the Teensy serial monitor send **g** over a uniform patch of field carpet, **w** over a white sample large enough to cover the whole ring, then **r** for raw channel differences. Calibration is held in RAM. Pin 39 switches illumination; dark minus lit cancels a steady ambient component while the receiver remains within its range. Pins 34–37 are address bits from least to most significant. Pins 14 and 40 read the two outputs sequentially at each address. The sketch reports frame time, a mask of weak calibration channels and the estimated center of each separate contact arc. It interpolates the two half-contrast crossings from calibrated neighboring sensors. A weak neighbor falls back to a half-pitch boundary. Opposite stripe contacts can coexist. Use the robot’s prior direction of travel to distinguish entering from leaving a boundary. White field markings elsewhere on the pitch can also trigger the array.

Run this test on green carpet and white tape under expected lighting, at several speeds, with motors and camera active. Sweep nominal 1–3 mm optical gaps, then measure pitch and roll under acceleration. Log missed crossings and false retreats by approach angle. Scan time is only part of stopping distance. At normal speed v, control latency t and measured deceleration a, reserve at least v·t + v²/(2a) of travel before collision. A sensor ring at radius 55 mm sees the stripe after the front of a larger robot reaches it. Tune speed near the line from measured stopping margin.

## Motion and power
The main schematic groups drivers and matching encoders by motor, with separate blocks for battery sense, vision, line readout and the referee interlock. Controller pin 41 reads the active-low shared G2 fault line; the onboard LED is on pin 13. Five buffered quadrature pairs enter the Teensy at 3.3 V from encoders powered at 5 V. J3–J7 carry G2 logic. J8–J12 carry corresponding encoder +5 V, GND, A, B. Check motor/encoder direction and channel pairing one wheel at a time. J19 exposes GND, heartbeat, RUN_OK and fault for a scope. The connection tables give every pin in mating order.

U1 combines referee PLAY and pin 33 (arm). A low at either input clears U4. While armed, rising edges on pin 12 retrigger U4. Its output drives the common SLP line. Hold heartbeat and PWM low before arming. The supplied DriveSupervisor.h implements STOP-before-PLAY, a 5 ms limit on both command age and the interval between active control-loop updates, and fault latching. Call begin() at boot and service() before applying PWM. Pass a genuine validated command timestamp and combined sensor-health status. The supervisor emits a pulse at most once every 2 ms from successful foreground updates. After a missed deadline it requires a new referee STOP followed by PLAY. An autonomous PWM peripheral could continue ticking after a software stall. At J19, RUN_OK is the combined referee and arm signal. Probe J3 pin 4 for the actual timed enable output. R7 is 100 kΩ and C8 is 100 nF X7R (KEMET C0805C104K1RACTU). Tolerance, temperature and trigger timing affect pulse width. The components set a nominal timeout on the order of 10 ms; measure minimum and maximum on the prototype. Test STOP during a frozen loop and test resuming PLAY with heartbeat held both high and low. The timing chip can also trigger on clear release with B high. Require fresh control state and zero PWM on restart. Fit the ten local SLP pull-downs listed for the two robots. Verify each driver stays asleep when its cable disconnects. A shared fault identifies a fault on at least one driver; identify the motor from independent current channels and service tests.

Use the 3S pack with the 30 A main fuse near the pack, first pole of the DC-rated master disconnect, four individually fused 3 A drive branches, one 7.5 A dribbler branch and the 2 A 5 V regulator branch. Motor current stays in the external harness. Each driver receives local bulk capacitance, its motor pair and a shared signal ground. Monitor current and limit stall duration; the default G2 current limit exceeds the motor's stall current. Feed J18 battery sense from the switched fused bus. Regulated 5 V feeds J13 (pin 1 positive), the camera VIN and the ring via J16. G2 CS and FLT are signals. The official referee module has an independent 1 A fused battery feed; route its OUT1 through the second isolated pole of the master switch to J17 pin 2 and use a common ground at J17 pin 1. This disconnects the signal from the unpowered Teensy when the robot is off. J14 pin 2 goes to N6 P5/RX, pin 3 to N6 P4/TX, pin 1 GND. Isolate Teensy VIN/VUSB before concurrent USB and external power. Verify the separate fused supply to the referee module against its exact revision and selected VIN pin before applying pack voltage. Test with the complete harness before connecting motors. Pololu’s MinIMU remains on the 3.3 V I²C branch. Check heading error with the drivers running. There is no powered kicker circuit.

## CAD and mechanical fit
Open **cad/drive-layout-study.step** for the four drive envelopes around the populated boards. **cad/electronics-stack.step** isolates the boards, seven spacers and insulating carrier. **cad/carrier.step** is the individual 2 mm plate. The PCB exports include the packages available in the footprint libraries. The controller and camera modules, plugged cables and load-bearing robot chassis require separate models.

The ring retains the supplied STEP profile and the main board uses the FeatureScript outline with four Ø3.0 mm holes on an 89.095 mm square. The carrier joins those four holes to three on the ring. ### Wheels and motor mounts

The selected Pololu 20D #3486 drive motors have 41 mm bodies and 4 mm D shafts. Use #3499 encoders, #1138 brackets and #18023 hubs for the 48 mm omni wheels. Retain the 25D #4841 and its separate bracket for the dribbler. The smaller drive motors free space near the center and reduce stall current. At 12 V the wheel speed limit calculated from 550 rpm unloaded and 48 mm diameter is 1.38 m/s. Forward robot speed, starting torque and braking distance require measurement with the battery, carpet and full robot mass. ### Motion calibration and bring-up

The existing BallAlgo settings assume a 25 mm wheel radius and 1620 rpm. Start the new drive at 24 mm and 550 rpm unloaded. With 25:1 gearing and 20 quadrature counts per motor revolution the nominal wheel scale is 500 counts/revolution, or 0.302 mm/count at the tread. With the wheels raised, associate each motor and encoder with its actual socket and check count and motion signs. The proposed four rolling axes are tangent to the radial shafts. Then measure loaded rollout and record the measured scale. The old 25 mm radius would overestimate distance by 4.17%.

### Commission the assembled robot

1. Load **firmware/LineBench/LineBench.ino** on the Teensy, or flash the matching Teensy 4.1 HEX. Use the green and white calibration procedure above.
2. Load **firmware/OpenMV/main.py** on the OpenMV N6 using OpenMV IDE. The script finds the largest orange candidate and sends its image coordinates, pixel area, frame sequence and CRC-8 over UART3 at 115200 baud. Tune the starting LAB threshold with the passive orange ball under field lighting. Fit the mirror mask and camera center using images of the actual chassis. A large orange object can produce a false detection. [N6 camera and UART reference](https://docs.openmv.io/dev/openmvcam/quickref/openmv-n6.html)
3. Install **firmware/MainBench/MainBench.ino** or its HEX on the Teensy. Open the USB serial monitor at 115200. Spin each wheel and roller by hand. Read all five encoder counts, five raw current channels, pack voltage, referee input and driver-fault state. The camera field distinguishes stale data, a fresh frame with no candidate, and candidate coordinates. Send **i** to probe the IMU addresses while the gate test is idle.
4. With wheels raised, verify the hardware gate on J3 pin 4 with a scope. While the referee input reads STOP, send **t** and then apply PLAY within three seconds. The software requests enable for the rest of that window with all five PWM outputs at zero. Send **s**, apply STOP, or unplug the referee signal to end it sooner. Test one driver with its battery branch connected before repeating for all five. The printed gate status is the software request. Measure the actual output, each driver's SLP voltage, the timeout after a frozen CPU and behavior through a power cycle.
5. Run the field tests before increasing speed. Match play still needs integrated motion control with encoder feedback, current limits, mirror-calibrated bearings and line-aware braking. The former CM5 executable and camera calibration are specific to that hardware.

Both Teensy sketches were compiled and linked for Teensy 4.1. The host tests exercised the line algorithm, stop logic and both ends of the camera packet format. Physical sensor performance, reboot behavior and the camera lens/mirror alignment still need a prototype.

## 2027 Soccer Vision inspection
The official Soccer League Committee publication checked November 23, 2026 is the **November 21 draft**. Under §6.2.1 the extended robot must fit an 180 mm cylinder, height 180 mm, with a maximum 15 mm ball-capture depth. Check all rollers and covers in the operating position. Sections 3.4–3.5 call for a handle with 50 mm hand clearance and an accessible white top marker at least 40 mm in diameter. Keep both clear of the camera view. Section 7.1 calls for a protected referee module at least 10 mm in from the outside at international events. Check the final international rules and the published supplement for the U.S. event. The draft suggests 3 mm of diameter margin for inspection. The current wheel model leaves 1.91 mm. Under §2.8 wall contact or moving fully inside the penalty area can incur an out-of-bounds penalty. White detection is an early control cue. The draft discusses a provisional black pushing line. Keep black/white classifications distinct. Mask exposed indicator LEDs and confirm that emitted light or body colors do not interfere with other vision systems (§3.1).
